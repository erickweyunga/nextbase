File fields take `thumbs`, up to 20 thumbnail sizes such as `300x200` (crop from the centre), `300x200t` and `300x200b` (from the top or bottom), `300x200f` (fit inside), `0x200` and `300x0` (one side), with sides up to 2500; `100x100` is always allowed. `GET /files/...?thumb=300x200`, or `nb.files.getUrl(record, name, { thumb })`, serves a JPEG, PNG, GIF or WebP image in that size, made with sharp on the first request, once per instance at a time, and stored beside the file under `thumbs_<name>/`, with the same access checks, cache headers and bucket redirects as the file. Thumbnails keep the image's format, are never enlarged, follow its EXIF orientation, leave out its metadata, and show the first frame of an animated GIF. Sizes are canonical: no leading zeros, and `t`, `b` or `f` only when neither side is 0. Other sizes, other types of file, files over 50 MB, images that cannot be read, have more than 50 megapixels, a side over 16384 or an aspect ratio over 50:1, requests that would wait too long for a free slot, and servers without sharp get a 302 to the file's own URL without `thumb`, with `Cache-Control: no-store`. Images whose header shows they can never be thumbed get a `.failed` marker beside the file so no instance reads them again; a size whose resize fails is tried again after 5 minutes. Both are logged as warnings with `data.type` `thumbs`; other errors loading sharp than its absence are logged as errors. Thumbnails are deleted with their file, record or collection. sharp is an optional peer dependency of `@nextbasejs/core`: install it to make thumbnails.
