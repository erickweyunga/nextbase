NextBase keeps a log of HTTP requests and background failures in the database, for 7 days by default. Each request gets one entry with its method, URL (with `token`, `code` and `state` redacted), status, time taken, the caller's collection, user agent, referer and, when it failed, the error, its field errors and, for unexpected errors, the stack. Bodies, cookies, the Authorization header and Postgres error details are never recorded. After hooks, file deletes, emails, texts, realtime cleanup and unexpected OAuth2 sign-in errors are logged at error too, and an OAuth2 sign-in that fails and redirects back is logged at warn with its error code. Entries are written in the background, a burst at a time with one insert at a time per instance, and never fail a request; `nextbase.flush()` writes those still waiting, for use before ending a pool or exiting. Superusers read them through `GET /logs`, `GET /logs/{id}` and `GET /logs/stats` with filters over `level`, `message` and `data.*`, or `nb.logs` in the client, and clear them with `DELETE /logs`. The new `logs` option sets `maxDays`, `minLevel`, `authId` and `ip`, or turns logs off with `false`. The new `clientIp` option says where the client's IP comes from: Vercel's headers on Vercel by default, a header your proxy sets with `trustedHops`, or a function. The OAuth2 callback answers 404 for a provider that is not set up, instead of failing with a 500.
